It feels surreal to Haymitch that he’s looking forward to Declan’s visit. Just a few months ago, he never would have believed that was possible.

He still feels nervous, of course. That kind of wound doesn’t heal with a single kiss to the forehead and a promise of continued love. But mostly, he feels... excited. Eager. He wants to see his father, because today might be hell—but his father won’t be. His memories of Declan’s last visit are not perfectly intact, but what he has of them are marked by gentleness and that repeated reassurance that he has never been without his father’s love.

His life as a whole is arguably marked by gentleness right now—everything about prison and his pain is defined by a *lack* of gentleness, a *lack* of care, that makes those times when someone chooses to be gentle with him shine all the more brightly.

His cell opens and his father enters, and he has to fight back a grin. A GALPOL officer accompanies Declan, then takes up his own place by the door to keep watch. 

Haymitch desperately wants to be in his father’s arms, but that could get their visit cut short—so he holds himself back and offers Declan only a small smile.

“Da. I’m so glad you’re here.”

☆☆☆☆

“Why the hell are you here?” 

Erik can barely make himself look at the man who now fills the doorway to his cell to ask the question. That man’s red hair is—to him—pigmented by blood of his family. The shape of that man’s hands and the contours of his knuckles curl, in Erik’s mind, around the grip of the rifle that had filled his sister with lead and left  her neck and torso filled with an entire magazine’s worth of bullet holes.

The man glances over his shoulder, then steps into the cell and closes the door behind him. Wordlessly, he takes a seat in the chair at the desk beside Erik’s bed. 

“To see you.”

Erik’s lip curls. 

“I have nothing to say to you.” He turns his face away from the man. He cannot turn his body; there is still far too much pain. They’ve been working for the past week to figure out where that pain is coming from, but despite reconstructing his cybernetics, the pain lingers, deep in his bones. It makes it hard to even turn his head, let alone his torso. He can still remember this man’s hand tearing back his chest to rip the explosive charge inside of him free.

He wishes the agent beside him had left it. He wishes he had been allowed to just end.

“I understand that.” He hears the man exhale. “But—I don’t need you to say anything. I asked after you; they told me that you’ve seemed... bored. Angry. I brought something to maybe help that.”

☆☆☆☆

His father smiles and sits down across from him.

“I figured you might be nervous about the hearing later, so I brought something to maybe help that.”

Haymitch raises an eyebrow as Declan pulls a small wooden board and a deck of cards out of his bag. 

“I assume your father taught you?” Declan asks as he sets up the cribbage board between them.

Haymitch smiles at him. Declan’s right—the anxiety has been getting to him, and this does, in fact, help.

“He did.” Haymitch reaches for the cards, sliding them from the box and thumbing the edge of one with his cybernetic hand. “I’ll warn you—I haven’t played in fucking ages. Might not remember how to peg.” He lets the joke rest for a single beat before letting himself grin and look up at Declan—and he finds that his father is rolling his eyes at him but *also* smiling.

“Incorrigible. Just like your father.”

“Oh? You’re incorrigible? Very self aware of you to acknowledge that.” Haymitch holds his eyes and continues to grin as he starts to shuffle the cards.

Declan rolls his eyes again, and Haymitch realizes that he’s never seen his father this relaxed before—not since he was a literal toddler, at the edge of his conscious memories. It feels, somehow, like going home—like somehow, in this moment, they are not in a GALPOL cell, but at a kitchen counter in a home that Erik and Declan no longer own, wrapped in the warmth of a family that has not known pain or death or lies.

☆☆☆☆

“You killed my family, and you expect me to play *cards* with you?” Erik’s words come out in almost a snarl, pain curling in his chest at the fucking *indignity*. 

The man stops, deck of cards in his hands, mid shuffle, stupid little board already placed between them.

“I expect you to take an opportunity for some variety.” The man resumes his shuffling. “Not to forgive me. You can hate me. You probably should.” He hesitates, then looks up at meets Erik’s eyes, as if the eye contact itself is painful to him. Erik’s lip twitches into a sneer, studying this man, this killer who took everything from him. Baby blue eyes, deep set. A close shaved beard that does not hide his round cheeks nor his light freckles. He is a boy playing at murder.

The man starts to deal the cards between them. Erik watches his hands. The man stops after dealing them each six, then looks up to hold Erik’s eyes again.

“You killed my family, too,” he says softly.

Erik lurches from the bed and punches him in the face.

☆☆☆☆

Haymitch deals the cards between them. It’s not proper technique to hand Declan each card individually, (”they’d shoot you in the old west,” as Erik used to say, whenever he dealt cards poorly as a teenager), but he wants the excuse to let his fingers brush against his father’s. He has been without this man for so long. He was never there to hold him as a child when he fell, never there to comfort him after a hard day at school, never there to clap him on the back at the milestones of his life. He has to make up for that lack of touch wherever he can, now.

He might be making up for it for the rest of his life, and somehow, he’s okay with that.

Declan accepts each card with a smile, until they each have six. They each take a moment of silence, examining their hands.

“Your kitty?” Declan confirms.

Haymitch grins. 

“I dealt. My kitty.” 

Declan nods, taking his time before selecting two cards, and handing them to Haymitch face down. Again, this isn’t standard; the cards for the kitty are typically placed on the table. They’re both stealing what small affections they can without getting their visit cut short.

Haymitch feels his own lip tug in the direction of a smile, but he makes himself focus on his cards. It's just nice to have someone who doesn't flinch away from his too-warm touch, but rather seeks it out. The only other person who really does that in his life is Liath. 

He selected his cards for the kitty and puts them face down on top of Declan’s. Every action feels familiar, from hundreds of games with Erik, and yet entirely new; he has never played their family's favorite game with Declan. He doesn't even have memories of his fathers playing it with each other. 

Granted, his memories of Declan from before have always been hazy and few. He remembers how Declan’s beard had felt against his face as a child. Those childhood recollections are now reinforced by the new memory of the kiss Declan had pressed to his forehead at the end of their last visit. 

It is still so strange to be able to play cards with a man whose grave he had visited every Sunday in his childhood. 

Declan reaches to cut the deck, then draws his hand back with a section of the cards to let Haymitch flip the fifth, common card for both of their hands. Cribbage has many peculiar little rituals built into it. It technically doesn't matter that the dealer flips the top card, but does not cut the deck—and yet the interplay is treated almost as sacred. Erik had definitely chastised him when he had taught him if he had tried to both cut and flip. 

Haymitch flips the six of hearts as the card to hold in common for both of their hands, and notices his father smile a bit. No poker face, then? He’d expected Declan to play cards with an impassive expression. That hint of a smile is strangely humanizing.

☆☆☆☆

For some unknown reason, the man is back the next day after Erik broke his nose. Erik is cuffed for his visits now.

“Why are you back?” Erik does not turn his face to look at him.

“To see you,” he answers. Infuriating bastard. Erik stares at the ceiling and scowls.

“I told you. I have nothing to say to you.”

“I know.” The man hesitates. Erik wishes he would just fucking leave. “I still have things to say to you.”

“Then say them, so that you can get out of my face and never see me again.” He still does not look at the man, but he hears him sigh.

“We never really met properly. I know your name from the files—but you don’t know mine.”

“I’m fine with that.” He doesn’t need the man who took everything from him humanized like that—not even by the scrap of connection a name would provide.

The man, however, gives him no such mercy.

“I’m Declan. Declan Falker.”

“Great. You’ve introduced yourself.” Erik exhales. “Fuck off, Declan Falker.” 

“I’m not done.”

“Well, too bad. I am.” Erik blinks at the ceiling. Its insulation, or soundproofing, or whatever, makes shapes to his drifting mind. He tries not to see the faces of his siblings in those shapes.

“I was hoping you’d tell me about your... your family.” 

Erik blinks hard, and finally looks over at Declan.

*“What?”*

The man holds his gaze for a moment before looking away.

“You—you said I killed your family. I told you that you killed mine.” Declan’s lip twitches slightly. A pained smile? It’s a strangely human emotion to see on the face of the man he has only associated with death and anger. “I realized that I want you to know who you killed, so... I figured you might want the same.” Declan’s eyes flick back to Erik’s. “Will you tell me about them?”

It’s Erik’s turn to hesitate.

“Did you bring that card game again?” he says finally.

Declan’s lip twitches again.

“Maybe. Why?”

“I prefer to keep my hands busy when I have to say hard things.” He holds out his cuffed wrists and jingles the links between them. “So. Did you bring the card, Falker?”

Declan’s lip twitches into that smile again for a brief moment. 

“I did.” He reaches to his belt and pulls out a set of keys, uncuffing Erik. “No breaking my nose again, yeah?”

“As long as you don’t say stupid shit,” Erik agrees. “Now. How do you play this game?”

☆☆☆☆

“I’ll start pegging,” Declan says. Haymitch can’t help but smirk. He always forgets how silly the terminology of this game is.

“Figure you’re pretty decent at that,” he says, grinning. Declan looks up at him, raising a single eyebrow only slightly. 

“Nine,” he says simply, placing the nine of clubs on the table.

Haymitch grins and places down his own nine of spades.

“Eighteen for two.” He watches his father’s smile widen just a hair as he hops his peg forward two.

“A good play.” Declan nods to their cards. “Or it would be. Twenty-seven for six.” He places the nine of hearts in front of him.” 

Haymitch stares—then laughs. 

“Fuckin’ hell. You are good at pegging.” He grins at Declan as his father takes his points. “Go.”

Declan nods. 

“Last card for one.”

☆☆☆☆

“...and then sometimes you get an extra point if you have the right jack,” Declan says. “So some people like to hang onto jacks extra. Like my father.” His lip twitches slightly at the word ‘father’, and not into his little half-smile of before.

Erik stares at the cards in his hands.

“So I’m making fifteens. Except when I’m making pairs. Except when I’m making runs. Except when I’m hoping the jack is ‘right.’”

Declan hesitates, then nods. 

“It... makes more sense when you’re playing.” 

Erik almost smiles—then forces his expression back to a scowl. Why the hell is he smiling at this bastard? Just because he has a stupid accent and apparently the patience to teach an equally stupid card game?

“Let’s play, then.” He takes the deck and starts to shuffle. “And I’ll tell you about the people you killed.”

He watches Declan’s face become serious once more, and he starts in on his reckoning of his dead.

“My sister is the one you decided to magdump. Her name was Daisy.” Erik deals six cards, as Declan had taught him a few minutes before. He looks up to Declan for confirmation, and receives a small nod in return. Declan does not interrupt his story.

“She liked poetry—particularly Emily Dickinson and Mary Oliver. She cried if the mouse traps we set in the kitchen caught anything, and when she could get away with it, she’d give them little funerals and read poetry over their bodies. She wrote some, too, but she never shared it. She kept it locked up, promising that we’d read it when it was... ready.” 

Erik finds that he’s close to crying.

“We’ll never read it now. They’re dead. My home is rubble. This is what you have done to me.”

☆☆☆☆

“Your go.” Haymitch notices that his father’s shoulders are actually relaxed, now. He’s never seen that before.

Haymitch look down at his cards nad picks out the six of spades.

“Six.”

“Sixteen.” Declan lays down the jack of hearts.

“Twenty-six.” His hearing still looms over him. The pain still clouds the back of his mind. His entire world, though, is the rhythm of the game and the soft presence his father seems to cast over the room.

*“Da?”* He reaches out telepathically.

*“Trying to cheat and see through my eyes?”* Declan’s reply is amused, and Haymitch blushes and ducks his head.

“Thirty-one for two.” Declan lays down his five of hearts, then looks up at Haymitch with a smile that contains no pain nor fear, despite the prison, despite the pain, despite the past.

Haymitch matches the smile.

“Told you that you were good at pegging.”

Declan smirks as he takes his points once more. 

“You’ve worn that joke out, lad. You only get one free pegging joke per game. The others have to be truly *good* pegging jokes.”

“Good pegging jokes? Like the sex y—” He suddenly remembers that this is his *father*, and probably *not* the person to make such a joke with. He ducks his head again.

Declan raises that eyebrow a tiny bit higher. 

“No, no. Finish your sentence.”

Haymitch coughs. 

“It. Was inappropriate.” 

Declan’s lip twitches. 

“Play your card and show me you can finish off pegging.” His expression remains *almost* completely neutral.

☆☆☆☆

Erik and Declan sit in silence for a long moment, letting Daisy’s death hang between them.

“I—I would have saved her, too, if I could have,” Declan says finally. His voice breaks.

Erik doesn’t want to believe him. But as he opens his mouth to tell him to go to hell, he remembers Declan’s touch. He remembers his last memory of that day, before pain and shrapnel had taken him mercifully into unconsciousness. He remembers Declan kneeling at his side, frantically hacking at his chest with a utility knife, trying desperately to get the explosive core out of his chest. Declan’s helmet had been broken by then. Erik had smashed it himself.

In his memory, Declan’s face was panicked as he worked, blood running down the side of his face, skin pallid from bloodloss sustained earlier in the raid. It is that panic that convinces Erik, at least in this moment, that the man in front of him is telling the truth.

He would have saved Daisy if he could have. He would have saved them all.

The tears come closer, and Erik inhales.

“Fair’s—fair’s fair.” He swallows hard, but the lump in his throat does not diminish. “You—you said I killed your family, too.” He looks up at Declan. “Will—will you tell me about them?”

☆☆☆☆

They do, in fact, finish pegging and move on to counting. 

Haymitch watches as his father counts his cards with a speed born of long practice. His hands seem to dance across the cards as he totals his points in the peculiar cribbage formula, like a spell—fifteen two, fifteen four, fifteen six, pair eight, right jack nine. The cadence is almost exactly the same as Erik’s would have been, counting those cards.

He has no memories of Declan singing lullabies to him as a child, but Erik had always told him that his Da had—Amazing Grace, Erik said, and a lullaby that as far as Erik knew, Declan had cobbled together for Haymitch.

“Da?” he asks, a question occurring to him suddenly.

“Mm?” Declan looks up from his counting. 

“Do—do you call Cydonia ‘Starbright?’”

Declan looks completely taken aback at the question. He takes a long moment to answer, carefully stacking his cards.

“I—I have not.” He hesitates, then looks up at Haymitch. “Until you... I had lost everyone I’d ever called Starbright, one way or another.”

Haymitch hesitates.

“I—I think you should.” He ducks his head. “I think it might like that.”

He coughs, then counts his own card, his own cadence slower. He is out of practice.

☆☆☆☆

“You killed Lawrence,” Declan says softly. The cards are almost forgotten between them. “He was the one whose neck you snapped. He—he was like an older brother to me. Terribly gentle. Teased me ruthelessly. He’s the reason I ever got confident enough to smile or joke when not—when not alone.” 

Erik nods, watching Declan’s face carefully. 

“I—I cannot say I would have saved him.” Erik swallows hard. “He—he threatened my family. I... I loved my family.”

Declan hesitates.

“And I take it you don’t have any outside of the cult?”

Erik hesitates, then shakes his head.

“I... no. Not anymore.” He frowns. “And they weren’t a cult. *We* weren’t a cult.”

Declan does not press the issue, although Erik can see his expression darken for just a moment.

“And you?” Erik presses. He’s not sure why he asks, only that it feels unfair not to. “Do you have... family outside the...” He waves a hand at Declan’s GALPOL uniform. “Cult?”

He watches Declan’s expression carefully and sees his lip twitch. He cannot tell if it would be into a smile or a scowl if Declan would let himself show the emotion.

“I have parents on Terra.” Declan hestitates. “They are technically family, insofar as anyone can be family who makes you feel like you don’t belong in your skin.”

Erik nods once, then looks down at his cards.

“So I want fifteens, yeah? Can I use more than two cards to make a fifteen?”

☆☆☆☆

Haymitch ends up with a hand of twelve points. The kitty, though, has a full fourteen. 

Declan actually whistles, then smiles up at Haymitch. 

“Guess I made the wrong choice throwing you that three and four.” His father is smiling, though. If Haymitch could bear the thought, he might have assumed the expression was pride. But it’s hard to imagine that Declan could be proud of him, here, as he is: in prison, skin yellowing like wax, touch hot enough to make people recoil. 

“I’ve forgiven you for worse choices.” The words leave his mouth before he can truly consider, and he ducks his head. “I—I’m sorry, I mean—”

Declan quickly shakes his head, putting a hand over Haymitch’s. His father does not flinch at the heat this time.

“I deserve all such censure.” Declan’s voice is quiet. “If you need to make such jokes for another year, I will not hold them against you. If you need to make them for another five, another ten—”

Declan looks up and holds his eyes for just a moment.

“I was gone for longer,” he says finally, voice barely audible. “As long as you know I’m trying to stay, now—say what you need to say, just try to stay with me.”

☆☆☆☆

They pass the rest of Declan’s visit like that. They trade stories of their dead. They pass the small details back and forth to one another like they are fragile flowers. They are the only ones who hold them, now. It feels important that there be another who knows what has been lost.

Even if that other is the man who took them.

By the time Erik has finished recounting his fallen siblings, the lump in his throat feels as large as the explosive Declan ripped from his chest the week before. Declan’s eyes are red and puffy by the time he finishes recounting his “squad.” Erik’s only memories of Declan’s family are as armored, unknowable foes—but he can tell, from the way Declan talks, that they truly *were* this boy’s family.

He is a boy. He’s twenty-one—two years younger than Erik himself. Neither of them should be without family. 

They continue the game with fewer words. Declan teaches him the “correct” way to count his points. It seems stupid to Erik. He follows every instruction that Declan gives him to the letter, anyway. 

This is what they have, now. Their families are gone. The only other who knows their stories sits in this room, a stupid fucking board and stupid fucking cards between them, blood on both of their hands, only one of them free to even have his wrists uncuffed when not supervised.

They finish the game. Declan wins easily. Erik doesn’t care.

“They said I have to cuff you again,” Declan says apologetically.

Erik nods and holds out his wrists. Declan stands and comes over to him, fastening the cuffs with more gentleness than any GALPOL officer has ever shown him.

“Thank you for letting me speak,” he says quietly as he locks the cuffs.

Erik looks up at him, studying his face.

“Thank you for letting me speak as well.”

Declan nods. 

“I—I’ll leave you your peace now, Erik.” He hesitates. “I wish you well—wherever you go.”

Erik feels a sudden panic.

“I—Decl—Falker.” He swallows, grasping for an excuse. Why the hell does the idea of never seeing this man again terrify him so much?

The answer comes easily. This is the only other person alive who ever saw Erik’s family, and who now knows their names. He may be an officer. He may be a murderer. He may be awkward as all hell.

But he’s arguably all Erik has left.

He swallows hard.

“I liked the game. You—you could come back and bring it with you again, sometime.” He hesitates, then shoves down his pride. He cannot lose the last link he has to his family yet. “I’d like that.”

He sees that unknowable flicker at the corner of Declan’s mouth again, and then he nods.

“Then I’ll come back.” He hesitates. “Take care, Erik.”

“You too, Falker.”

☆☆☆☆



Haymitch swallows hard.

“I—I told Liath and Cy and Caroline that I’m living for them.” He looks up at Declan. “I—I can live for another person, too.”

He sees Declan’s face twist for a moment into something he truly can’t read. He opens his mouth to speak, then closes it again.

“Thank you,” is all he says finally. Then, wordlessly, he takes the deck, scoops the counted cards between them, and shuffles to begin the next round.

They continue the game with fewer words. Their counting creates a rhythm between them that is easier than explanation.

This is what they have, now. The men they one were—even the men they were a year ago—are gone. Haymitch thinks he could miss the man he had been a year ago. That man, though, had been so much more naïve—and that man had not had his father back from both the dead and from resentment.

*No,* he decides as he watches his father easily win the game. *I would not trade who I am now for the man I was then, even though he did not carry this pain nor this guilt.*

The man he is today has something far more precious than health or innocence: he has family.
