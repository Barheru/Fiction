Declan paces nervously in the waiting room of the HGI cloning facility.

Haymitch is going to be fine. His son is going be fine—or, as fine as he'd been before. Declan has packed spare mannitol and stubantazine for Haymitch, along with a handkerchief for his nosebleeds, and a water bottle, and an ice pack, just in case his temperature was still too high, and a comfy sweater, in case he’s somehow cold instead, and—

*Having children is somehow more painful than being apart from them.* 

He forces his breathing into an even, steady rhythm. In spite of all the fear, he has not been failing his children. 

His... children. 

Moira has called him “dad” twice, now. He would never have made that choice for her; he would never have asked that of her. 

*You've failed so many of the young people you've tried to take under your wing.* He pushes the thought away. That had been before; that had been when he’d been trying to fill the gaping wound left in his life by the separation from his son. That isn't the case with Moira. He's about to take Haymitch home, after all. His son is his once more, and yet there is still room in his life for the girl out there who called him “dad.”

He's not going to fail her, nor is he going to fail Haymitch. He is finally going to be the father his children deserve. 

The door to the cloning chamber finally opens, and Haymitch steps out, wrapped in a thin hospital gown. Declan stares at him—the leaps to his feet, rushing to his side, taking his son’s hands in his and turning them over in his own.

Haymitch is not hot to the touch. 

He stares in disbelief at the man before him—then pulls him into his arms.

The Haymitch he had brought to the station that morning, and delivered to Moira for—for their ritual—had been more than visibly unwell; he had been dying. His skin had had an unnatural golden hue to it; his eyes had been jaundiced; his cheeks had had a strange, waxy sheen to them that only accented their hollowness. 

The man whom he holds in his arms has flushed, rosy cheeks, and blue eyes that are almost perfect mirrors of Declan’s own—although they are less clouded by age. His skin is warm with the warmth of life, and not of barely contained fire.

“You *live,*” Declan whispers, cupping his son’s face in his hands. He’s not talking about the successful cloning.

Haymitch looks up at him with an emotion that Declan cannot place—and out of habit, born of that week of sharing his son’s mind in order to try to share some part of his fears, Declan opens that mental door between them and tries to catch a glimpse inside.

Haymitch’s mind is calm. It is cool; always before, even when his son was conscious and not fighting with the shadows in his mind, Haymitch’s thoughts have had a sense of eerie warmth to them. That warmth is gone, now.

A sudden wave of trepidation passes over him. He should have asked before opening that door and looking inside.

Haymitch, however, shakes his head slightly in response to that anxiety.

*“I live, and it is in great part thanks to you.”* Haymitch does not lift his face from Declan’s shoulder. Declan clings to his son’s unspoken reply just as much as he clings to his shoulders, trying not to sob. His words are not warm. His mind is not warm. The fire has retreated.

*“I was so worried that it had hurt you.”* His words make Declan choke with some combination of laughter and disbelief. 

*“You were worried that it had hurt* me*?*” He runs his hands through Haymitch’s hair and does not think about the yellow blood that he had washed out of the knees of his pants.

Haymitch nods against him again.

*“I—I don’t remember much of—but I remember that you tried to keep me safe, and it did not always take kindly to that.”* Haymitch hesitates, looking up at his father. *“You were there so much of the time. I don’t remember much. But I remember that.”*

Declan trembles and does not reply. What can he say? That this is the least he can do? That he can only begin to offset years of absence in a single week of care and presence?

*“A single week of pure terror. Don’t downplay what you did.”* His son’s words in his mind—a reply to thoughts that had never become fully verbal—startle him, but he does not push Haymitch from his mind.

*“I have questions about what I saw in that fear, in you,”* Haymitch adds. Declan can feel the hesitation in that thought. There are stories Haymitch glimpsed, then, that he remembers enough of to have questions.

*“In time. I’ll give you answers, I promise,”* Declan replies. *“For today, just—just come home.”*

Haymitch finally pulls out of his arms and smiles up at him.

“I am so, so ready to go home.” He then hesitates. Declan feels the anxiety wash through him, and by habit of that week, he draws some of it into himself, off of Haymitch’s shoulders. Haymitch raises one eyebrow at him, just slightly, but does not cut him off from his mind.

“Moira is safe, too,” Declan says, answering the unspoken question that had prompted the anxiety. “It was not—easy on her. Physically. But I left her with her friend and by now, she is back in her regular body. I did not leave her until I knew she was safe.”

Haymitch smiles slightly, not speaking. Declan knows what he’s feeling, though—and it almost makes his knees weak.

Haymitch is proud of him for his care for Moira—and proud to call Declan his father.
