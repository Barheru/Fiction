Sergeant Faith Eleazar receives the body bag at the GALPOL prisoner holding facility. Her eyes flick over the label—Jacob Edmunds. Human. Unrevivable via on station cloning technology; he’ll need a full backup pulled from their database. She moves to the cloning pod and pulls up his file. He’ll print fully in a few hours, with memories complete to the point where he had been sent off to that last corporate station where they’d been careless enough to let him get stabbed that badly.

She goes back to the body bag and unzips it, looking down at the face inside—and freezes. 

She turns back to the report. What had the damage said? It was physical, right? Lacerations and asphyxiation, a bit of poison, not—

She checks over the records, then looks back at the body in the bag in shock.

It’s burned. Not just singed—charred. The skin is blackened. Sections of it slough off, revealing flesh that is fully blackened underneath. In several places, the bones themselves are visible; they, too, are blackened, as if they had been in a fire pit.

He looks like he’s already been incompletely cremated.

She swallows hard. She just—she’ll just report this in the system. Someone back at that station—Hestia Galactic Incorporated, was it?—someone there must have made a mistake in the damage type on the report. That’s—that’s the only explanation.

She logs this, then goes to transfer the body into the crematorium. It crumbles at her touch, and she recoils.

It’s not worth it to try to move it. She zips the body bag and throws the entire thing into the crematorium. She’d rather get scolded for losing one bag than have to touch whatever... *that* is, in it. One of those will be a lot easier to sleep through.

She latches the crematorium shut and flips the switch, then returns to the cloning pod to check its progress. Nothing is visible yet, of course. The amino acids that will build his cells are still assembling. 

There’s a peculiar warning light on, though. She frowns. Temperature? How the hell is the temperature wrong on the cloning pod? She checks it and feels herself do yet another double take. It’s almost fifty degrees Celsius higher than it should be.

She manually adjusts the temperature, overriding the recommended settings. She’ll get a technician out to look at the machine later.

The room feels too warm. Her skin itches a little under her fur. 

She’s going to be really glad to be done with this print.
