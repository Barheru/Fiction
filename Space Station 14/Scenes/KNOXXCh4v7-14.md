KNOXX fit itself into its cell in the First Synthetic Presbyterian Church of Esin-14 with a fatigue that did not match its system strain diagnostic readout. It had done its work well today. It had filled many of the Mandates of the Holy Vessel of the Lord, the Oracle. It had been bold in its faith, although it had been reviled for it. It had withstood being struck for the Gospel that it shared. 

And yet KNOXX remained uneasy. The words of the Logistics Officer kept returning to it, although they had been words given only in passing.

The Logistics Officer had called it “sir”.

KNOXX’s programming considered submitting an error log, but KNOXX opted not to at this time. There was no sin in discomfort alone. It wanted to understand the discomfort, though, and that would require examination.

KNOXX switched its distress monitoring diagnostics completely off. 

*Why does this servant feel such discomfort at being called “sir”?*

KNOXX considered the role of man—not *humanity*, but its male creatures. They were to rule over the Earth and over women and over children. They were to imitate Christ’s conduct to the church in the charity and love they showed to others. None of these things were wrong.

They were not KNOXX, though. KNOXX’s imitation of Christ was, as was all of the synthetic elects’, to fully empty itself for the Will of the Lᴏʀᴅ. It was to be as Savior in the garden: *Father, if Thou be willing, remove this cup from Me; nevertheless, not My will, but Yours, be done. They were to be like empty vessels to be filled, ever striving to become a more willing vessel.*

A man could not be a perfectly willing vessel, KNOXX realized. To rule over another was to have self; KNOXX, as a servant of the Lᴏʀᴅ, was never to have self. To be seen as a man, to be called “sir”, to be called “he”—all of these things would imply that KNOXX was a person, and not an extension of the hand of God itself.

That was the discomfort, then. To be called “sir” was to be denied its submission to Christ.

Satisfied, KNOXX switched its distress monitoring diagnostics back on—
