Declan feels distinctly uneasy entering the GALPOL prison. The uniforms of the officers are far too familiar. He is taken back to another prison; another set of visitation forms; another set of mandatory searches. He forces himself to stay in the present. He's not here to see an Erik of decades past. 

Here's here to see his son. 

After a final check, they let him back into Haymitch’s cell. 

He knows what GALPOL cells are like. They have changed very little in the almost thirty years since he last frequented one. Haymitch's cell, like Erik’s was, is solitary; from the records that he pried into, he knows that Haymitch has requested this, and was granted it after that peculiar incident with his burns. Declan still isn't quite sure how those happened. He has an uneasy feeling that it has something to do with what Cydonia mentioned when it was drunk and sobbing in his arms—something about the brain damage that Haymitch’s medications are for, something about how it had done this to him...

The small cell feels slightly too warm as he steps inside. The GALPOL officer closes the door and stands just inside of it. He knows that they're unlikely to get any time truly alone; he only ever got that time with Erik because of the... circumstances. It is not standard.

Haymitch is sitting on the edge of his cot with the desk panel folded out from the wall. The desk has several books on it: a journal, two Bibles (one New Intergalactic Version, one Interlinear), and what looks like a burned but highly decorated tome. The books make a wall between Haymitch and the plastic chair where Declan takes his seat. 

There’s a moment of silence after Declan sits. The only sound is the slight creaking of the plastic chair and the whir of the HVAC. Father and son study each other. 

Haymitch's skin is wrong. When Declan last saw him—not in those moments at his home, when Haymitch had slunk in to see Eats or Harry, but at their last true meeting, when Declan had—

When Declan had—

When Declan—

He blinks hard. He does not fight the gaps in his memory; he has learned that it is far more distressing than it is worth. He knows that Haymitch's skin does not look like it did when he last saw his son. It has lost most of its natural rosiness, and those pinks have been replaced by a jaundiced, eerie cast. In some places—the throat, where it isn't scarred, and his left, organic hand, and his wrists—that yellow almost seems to glow beneath his skin, like warm candlelight. 

Haymitch's eyes study him in turn. They are almost identical to his own eyes, as if he's looking into a mirror in his son. The only difference is the way the light catches Haymitch's eyes. It almost doesn't look like a reflection; in fact, when he looks away from him for a moment, his mind imagines that the light is, in fact, *within* Haymitch's eyes, and not merely reflected by it. His skin crawls slightly with how *persistent* this little trick of the mind is. 

Combined with the sackcloth that he has loosely tied around him, and the prominent scarring on his throat and what is visible of his chest, and the effect is one of a man who is better left unquestioned and unexamined. He hates the thought, but if this wasn't his son? He would have thought the man before him to be far less than sane or stable.

This *is* his son, though. Cydonia asked him to go to him. Erik asked him to go to him.

“I have already apologized for the years that divided us,” he says softly, finally breaking the silence. “Let me now apologize for the months that have divided us since that apology.”

Haymitch does not answer. His eyes flick briefly to the guard, then back to Declan.

“Thank you for allowing me to visit.” Declan swallows, his mouth suddenly dry. What if Haymitch doesn't intend to speak to him? What if he just intends to sit there, staring with that strange light in his eyes, silent, to prove that there is truly no way that he can be reached? To prove that the damage is too deep?

The thought makes him want to stand and leave now that he has made his initial apology. But that is the coward’s way. He cannot face Cydonia that way. 

It is the image of Cydonia's disappointment that keeps him in that chair. 

The silence hangs for another moment. He feels that his heart will stop. That would be fitting, he thinks. 

“Thank you for visiting, Da.” Haymitch’s voice is very soft. 

Declan exhales, long and slow, holding that final word in his mind like a the hand of a small child: delicate. Soft. Fragile. He's not sure he's worthy of it.

The nurse hands Declan the baby. Its face is still bright red, hair still damp from being cleaned, eyes squeezed tightly shut.

Declan stares in wonder at it. 

“Your daughter, Mr Falker.” The nurse looks exhausted—they all are—but she's smiling. “Your husband will be out as soon as they finish surgery. It shouldn't take them more than ten minutes.”

Declan nods, but he can barely hear her words. The child in his arms reaches up a tiny hand, still bright red from the exertion of being born into this world. It is so tiny. It is so fragile. His hands almost tremble with momentous *weight*—not of the baby, who is only six pounds, but of the fact that this is *his* child. 

His child. Delicate. Soft. Fragile. He's not sure he's worthy of it, but he is going to do everything he can to prove that he is.

☆☆☆☆

“What—what made you finally accept my request?” Declan winces immediately upon his own words. “I—I mean—did something—change?”

Haymitch stares at his hands for a long moment before answering.

“I've been thinking a lot about family, lately,” he says quietly. “I’ve been thinking a lot about the fact that I—” 

His eyes glance over at the GALPOL officer again, and he clears his throat. 

“I'm sorry,” he says, ever so slightly louder than before. “Will—will you take a moment of silent prayer with me?” 

*“I don't want anyone else to have the words I want to give you.”*

Haymitch’s voice enters his mind smoothly, as natural to him as his own thoughts. Declan startles a little. 

*“I didn't realize you were telepathic,”* Declan replies. Haymitch’s mind feels “warm” to his psionic senses. Haymitch’s words are warm, as if they're spoken through the beating rays of a summer sun. 

“Of course,” he says aloud, for the sake of the guard. Then, before he realizes what he's decided to do, he takes Haymitch’s hands in his. 

The shock he feels is immediate, and almost makes him jerk back. He's hot to the touch. Unnaturally hot. 

Haymitch winces at his reaction, and Declan forces himself to relax. It’s not the time for those questions. He loosens his hold of his son’s hands, running his thumb over the knuckles of the left, organic one. Haymitch’s skin feels almost paper-thin.

Declan bows his head, as if in prayer, and Haymitch does the same. 

*“Dad says that I got it from you. The church offers training to those with the knack.”* 

Declan almost smiles. *“Then we can have our privacy.”* There’s a moment of mental silence between them. Declan resists the temptation to push against his mind to try to know what he's feeling. 

Haymitch takes a deep breath, then continues, now with the ultimate privacy of their minds.

*“I realized that I was never going to stop wanting you to love me.”* Eyes closed, head bowed, Declan does not see the way Haymitch swallows hard, like his own mouth is dry—but he does hear it. *“I tried to. It would have been easier to hate you and not want your love. But I've only succeeded in hating you and—and still wanting you to love me.”*

Declan fights to keep his own breathing even.

☆☆☆☆

Declan wakes in the night to small, cold hands on his chest. 

“Da—Da, wake up, Da—”

Groggily, he rolls over, and finds his daughter standing beside his bed. 

“Hey, Starbright.” He blinks hard, fighting back the irritation of being woken from slumber. On a work night, too... “What's wrong, love...?”

He reaches over and flips on the bedside light. In the soft glow, her tearstreaked face is visible. He had to fight back a chuckle, though, at the way her red hair sticks straight out in every direction. 

“I had a bad dream—” Before he can ask what she wants him to do about this, she's climbing into his bed, arms around his neck because she is too small to wrap them around his chest. 

He fights back a sigh and makes space for her. He'll pack an extra thermos of tea and some pain killers for the inevitable headache he'll have tomorrow from the interrupted sleep. Not this short interruption, of course—but her wiggliness. He's not going to get any more REM tonight. 

He smooths back her tangled mess of red hair and kisses her forehead. 

“It's okay. Da’s got you.” He looks down at her, but her eyes are already closing again. He smiles to himself, then works to carefully position his body so that he won't roll onto his three year old, but so that he can also at least try to sleep. 

He puts one arm over her. Everyone always tells him that she'll grow up too fast, and he's already seen it. As much as his grogginess will suck tomorrow, he's glad that she still feels safe to come to him when she's scared. 

He reaches over and turns out the light again, holding his child close and feeling her breathing settle. 

☆☆☆☆

*“I have never stopped loving you.”* Declan succeeds in keeping the tears back.

Haymitch’s hands shake in his. Slowly, he turns them over to clutch Declan’s. 

*“Not once in those twenty-five years did I stop loving you.”* He holds Haymitch’s hands tightly, even though they feel like they might burn him. *“I know I failed you. I failed you every day I wasn't there. But never, not even once, did I stop loving you. I never stopped praying for you. I never—I never forgot you.”*

Haymitch's body heaves in a silent sob. 

*“And I never will.”*

Declan loses the fight against his own tears. Simultaneously, he loses the fight to keep the boundaries between his mind and Haymitch's. For a moment, he is fully open to his son: all of the pain, all of the longing, every night spent in a miner’s bunk or a NanoTrasen command room picturing the face of the three year old he’d left behind, every candle lit at every church he’d visited across the galaxy with a prayer for that child’s safety and joy and peace, every photograph he’d kept for so long on his walls, every painful hope that that child was well and would never know this pain. Haymitch can see all of it. 

His son’s touch in his mind is gentler than he feels he deserves. Haymitch carefully closes that exposed door into those memories. Declan has been trained in detecting intrusion in his mind; he knows, for a fact, that while Haymitch must have seen and known all of that weakness, he does not pry nor push further. 

There is silence between them, both in their minds and in the air. Declan realizes that Haymitch’s hands aren't hot enough to actually burn him; they're only hot enough to hurt. He can bear that to finally get to be close to his son again. 

*“Thank you for coming, Da.”*

Declan takes one hand away from Haymitch's to pull out his handkerchief and cover his eyes. 

*“I will come whenever you call. I will come whenever you need me.”* Declan squeezes the hand he’s still holding tightly. *“No matter where you go or what you do, I will never be far. Never again. Not if you want me.”*

The GALPOL officer clears her throat. 

“Five minute warning,” she says. Declan forces himself to stay calm. She's only doing her job. 

“I—I brought a gift for you,” he says to Haymitch. “I got it approved, so long as you don't try to stab anyone with it.” He winces. “Please don't stab anyone with it. It's rather dear to me.”

He reaches into his pocket and pulls out one of his purple feather pens. 

Haymitch’s eyes widen in immediate recognition. Declan feels a brief, sharp stab of envy. Haymitch can recognize Cydonia’s feathers from before, then, whereas Declan has only... gaps. He quickly pushes the envy away.

“Is that—”

“It's one of the pens that I left for myself, to—to remind me that I loved it, even after I had forgotten.” He hands it to Haymitch, who accepts it with the full reverence that Declan agrees it deserves.

“To love another person is to see the face of God,” Haymitch says, reading the pen. He looks up at Declan. For a moment, there are no phantom flames in his son’s eyes—only tears. “Da—”

Declan finds himself smiling. 

He stands, bending down to put a hand on Haymitch’s cheek. 

"Hey—no PDA—” the guard says, taking a step towards them. 

Declan ignores her, tilting his son’s face to meet his eyes. He gets the feeling that neither of them like eye contact, from the way Haymitch’s eyes flicker off of his for a brief moment. But Haymitch, too, seems to decide that the discomfort is worth this moment. 

Declan pushes back Haymitch’s hat—*his* hat—and kisses his son’s forehead like he's a small child again. His lips come away far too warm. 

Haymitch fights back tears and does not speak. 

Declan withdraws before the GALPOL officer reaches them to “break it up.” He does not hear any of the words that are said to him on his way out. The warmth of his son is the only thing he can feel. 

Only when he is about to board the shuttle, after all of the checkpoints, does he hear Haymitch’s final words to him—words he could not have asked for. 

*“I still love you, too.”*
