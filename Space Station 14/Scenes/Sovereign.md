Haymitch barely remembers the crimes for which he now stands before the judge reviewing his time on parole.

He has given Declan all of his recent memories. It helps so much—it almost hurts him to admit how much it helps to have Declan hold those memories for him. It's time consuming. He spends at least an hour every evening giving Declan the day’s memories, and retrieving his own when he finds things fading. 

But his memories of his time since leaving Cyje 8 are remarkably clearer than those before. His memories since he abandoned his physical journal for the record held in his father's mind are as clear as they've ever been. He can remember Cydonia's face, even when a week or more separates them; he can remember slowly flirting his way into his relationship with Takes-the-Shells with all the butterflies of each small risk and seduction. 

But he does not remember the crimes for which he stands before the judge today.

Judge Cyprus Jachin reads over the charges. Haymitch does remember these—but he remembers them as empty facts, bereft of personal context. Four charges of murder. Two charges of smuggling. Illegal mercenary activity. These, to him, feel like his blood type (B-), or his hormone labs (332 ng/dL at last check), or his Solarian Benefits Number (6-24601-5-29). It is associated with him. It goes on papers with his name. But he has no experiential knowledge with it.

Which four murders did they have on file for him? He has no idea. He probably doesn't remember them. He doesn't remember most of them. He flexes his cybernetic right hand; how strange, to have suffered so greatly to learn how to kill, only to forget the killings.

Judge Jachin begins to read over his behavior reports from his time on prison.

“I'd also like to note,” he says, addressing the pair of GALPOL clerks and guard who are the only other people in the small courtroom, “that Mr. Falker has had no incident reports since his last parole hearing, despite working fairly regularly aboard Hestia Galactic Incorporated stations. Mr. Falker, do you have anything you wish to add, regarding your time on parole?”

Haymitch knows that his statement here is only meant to be brief. He's fine with that.

“I've been living with my father since my release,” he says quietly. Declan had helped him prepare his statement. “I have spent most of my time working as a bartender, or spending time with my family. I recently re-certified as a station engineer. I look forward to continuing to participate in making the places where I and my family work safer and more welcoming.” 

He hates that last part of the statement, but Declan was adamant—and what Declan insists on, Haymitch will follow. 

He would have hated giving anyone, *especially* Declan, that power, once upon a time. *What about self-sovereignty?* His younger, firebrand self demands of him. *What of freedom? What of staying true to one's beliefs?*

Judge Cyprus Jachin nods to him, and he takes his seat once more.

“In light of this statement, Mr. Falker’s prison record, and his behavior over the last two months, I rule that he is now released from parole. This hearing is concluded.”

Haymitch stares at his hands for a long moment—then stands. He nods briefly to Judge Jachin, then sticks his hands in his pockets and makes his way quietly out to the main lobby of the GALPOL courthouse. 

Self-sovereignty has always been a lie. He looks up at the vaulting, old Terran style dome of the courthouse above him, and shivers. No man walks alone; no man can, anymore. 

Haymitch feels that interconnectedness as chains sometimes. He feels it in the social web of money and power and poverty and hurt that got him here; he feels it every time he sees Security arrest someone who sold their sovereignty to the Syndicate, instead of the “right” corporation. 



But chains don't just bind bodies; they hold up bridges, too, and lower buckets into deep wells. His web is holds him to Liath and Caroline, Cydonia and Takes-the-Shells, and of course—to his memories by way of Declan.



Declan, who is waiting for him in the lobby, to take him home—finally a free man.
