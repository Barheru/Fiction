Haymitch wakes in pain.

In the moments before he is fully conscious, he experiences this pain primarily as anxiety. It pins him down like some kind of beast crouching over his chest, pressing down on his ribcage and trying to collapse his diaphragm. It is in these moments before he is truly awake that the pain is most difficult to deal with. His half-conscious mind has no faith to justify the suffering.

He slowly fights his way to consciousness. He has something he's holding on for. His mind knows this almost as assuredly as it knows the pain. 

*Cydonia. Liath. Declan.*

He exhales sharply as he finally forces himself to open his eyes. The low ceiling of the prison cell greets him, with a peculiar water stain in the shape of a lumpy phoenix almost directly over his head. He stares at it as he blinks himself further awake, pulling the blankets more tightly around him.

The loneliness is starting to get to him. The next time he sees Liath—welll, no, the next time he sees *anyone* who loves him, he needs to spend every moment of that visit with his body pressed as close against them as they'll let him. The loneliness has turned from psychological to physiological. The blanket is a poor substitute for loving touch. 

If he thinks about that for too long, he'll cry, so he puts it from his mind and stares at the misshapen firebird water damage on the ceiling again. Ironic, to look like a phoenix and yet be the mark of some leaking plumbing. A leaky pipe is probably the philosophical opposite of a pheonix—watery, unintentional, and a slow, slow deterioration. 

Is Cydonia technically a phoenix? He reaches under the rolled up sackcloth that he uses as a pillow, fumbling for the feather pen that is wrapped in it. He pulls it out and holds it up over himself, taking the shaft between thumb and forefinger, smoothing the vanes as he draws the feather through his fingers. It's from its old body—a body that is no more. 

He'd never expected to end up loving it like this, back when they had clung to each other and wept in that failed circle. He still doesn't know how he loves it—what he wants from that love, at least. That psychiatrist was right. Part of the reason that he feels like he is never met in turn when he reaches out is because he's always flinching back when an offer in kind is made. It simply doesn't feel fair to ask someone to love a dead man. 

That's why he hasn't reached out to Itzial since his arrest. He takes a deep breath as her name enters his mind, then releases it. She is safe; she is safe and even if he never hears from her again, her face won't fade from his memory. He has her picture. He hopes she still has his lighter and maybe even the name he gave her. It was, after all, a blessing. He wants her to keep that. 

It had been nice, loving a girl like that, but... he’s not sure it really could have been him. Dating Itzial had felt like a break from his life. He'd let himself dream of more than death; he’d imagined a future where he could have kids and a wife and maybe even a normal job. 

*I think that was a good thing.* He smooths the feather of Cydonia’s corpse again. *I think it was a good thing that I got to love her like that, even if that's not the kind of thing I can have.*

He puts the feather part of the pen against his lips, like it's silencing him, and continues to stare up at the ceiling.

The main thing he misses right now about Itzial was feeling like there was someone he could ask to hold him, without also feeling like a burdensome child. 

*No.* That's just a lie. He is not a burden to Liath; she has held him and told him that he is a good son. He fights to keep his breathing even. This will pass. This *will* pass. He's not fully awake yet, and when he is, the fear and the loneliness will subside—or at least he'll be distracted from it by new, fresh, mundane problems. For now, he just has to breathe through it, as he does through every pain. 

*Soretizone.* The itch is starting up, even as his migraine throbs dully. And yet he hesitates to take it. He hates giving in to that itch. He cannot let it grow. So he doesn't move to take his meds, yet; instead, he chooses to prove that he is master of those feelings, and he remains in bed, headache sending dull ripples of pain through his face and spine. He has to work to keep from clenching his jaw from it. He knows from experience that that will only make him sore later.

He wonders how Liath wakes. Lazily, he imagines. Some part of him is sure that if he fell asleep in her arms, he wouldn't wake up in pain. Maybe after she—helps him with his protections (he does not let himself dwell on that further)—he can convince her into letting him stay the night, wherever she is, and he can test that theory.

Admittedly, he's definitely going to wake up with *some* pain after that... encounter. He once again shoves those thoughts aside. They'll be fine. She insisted that she be allowed to help. And it—it won't hurt as much as last time. He's better at pain now, anyway. 

He's never really woken up with someone consistently. He never spent the night too often with Itzial because he knew they were *slightly* insufferable to Teresa—and he had liked his girlfriend's sister, and hadn't wanted to bother her. Waking up in Itzial’s wings, though, had been... It had been so safe.

He doesn't remember if he's felt that safe since then.

*Maybe after I get out, and once I'm back from Oxbow... I'll see if Da will have me.* He's pretty sure Declan would let him have the spare room if he asked. He's a little less certain if he wants to ask, but his father...

Well, he's still not fully sure what to feel for him. He can still feel the way Declan’s hand on his cheek and his lips on his forehead—he tries to recall them daily, so that they di not fade. He does not want the memory of that gentle touch to fade.

He knows that his father would be that gentle if he went to live with him. He's just not sure he wants to live in that gentleness. 

The pain decides he's been too complacent. It turns from that dull, familiar ache to sharper, more insistent stabs of flame. He fights to hold on a little longer, even as he knows the soretizone will hit beautifully. 

*Hold on for Cydonia.* It would not want to see him fall to addiction. *Hold on for Liath.* His mother has always been so careful with him and his meds. He still hasn't had a drop of alcohol in weeks. 

*Hold on for Kalymos.* The angel in its face had told him that this pain was his, now, because he had grown complacent. He has to wrestle with his trials, not just medicated them away, or he will never be refined. In this and this alone, he does not despise the name “Jacob”. Jacob can grapple with God.

He grits his teeth and focuses on his heartbeat through the pain, even as unwilling tears fill his eyes. The line from an old hymn floats back to him:

*He will put his strength in me*

*He knows the way I've stolled;*

*When I am tried sufficiently*

*I shall come forth as gold.*
